# Where GO ridership cannot show up yet

## The question

I asked which GO and UP stations have a strong surrounding travel market, but weak walk access, local connections, rail service, parking headroom, or fare. I did not ask which stations have low ridership. Station boardings and PRESTO transactions are not open, so a ridership model would have been a guess. The scorecard is the evidence I can defend, and it is the question I would take to Transit Strategy and Insights.

## What I did, in order

I first took the current GO and UP Express schedules for Wednesday 2026-11-18, the Saturday, and the Sunday inside that feed. I measured the AM headway toward Union, the longest midday gap, the weekday span from the first train to the last, and the weekend span. Trip counts stay in the table as evidence. They do not enter the service score. I then placed every local bus stop within 250 m of a station, using the latest open feed from each of 14 municipal operators, and measured the scheduled wait from an arriving bus to the next train toward Union. I then built two catchments. The walk catchment is the street network within 800 m. The station shed is the area closer to that station than to any other, capped at 10 km. I put 2021 Census population, jobs, recent housing, work from home, and the share of commuters whose usual workplace is Toronto into those catchments. For the fare condition I used a generalized cost: the published fare to Union, plus a value of time applied to the scheduled ride and to half the AM headway. The value of time is half the shed's median household income, spread over 2,080 hours. Only after that table was complete did I score the stations and name a primary likely constraint.

## The two analyses

Analysis 1 is a quadrant, not a cause. I scored 70 stations. Union Station is the network destination, and Stratford is outside the tracted census area, so I left both out of the ranking. The median market score is 50.7 and the median conditions score is 47.5. A station above the market median and below the conditions median is a priority: the market is ahead of the conditions. I also mark a station that sits within 5 points of either median, because that station would change class if the cut moved by a small amount.

Analysis 2 names the primary likely constraint for those priority stations. I compared each condition with the 20 stations that have both a strong market and strong conditions. The deficit is the number of points the station sits below that reference median. The largest deficit is the primary likely constraint. If the second deficit is within 10 points, I call it a tie and I do not recommend a single capital project. Land use is the label when recent housing growth carries the market score while the walk catchment is still thin.

Guelph Central is the station I can check on the ground. Its market score is 51 and its conditions score is 50.9. The conditions median is 47.5, so Guelph sits 3.4 points above the conditions cut. It is not in the priority set. It is within 5 points of a median, so I do not treat that side of the cut as a firm class. The largest deficit against the reference stations is still fare burden, at 63 points. The walk catchment has about 4,489 residents and 3,561 jobs, and the station shed has about 150,996 residents. The census subdivision sends 1.7% of its commuters to Toronto, and 26% of employed residents in the shed worked at home in 2021. The 2019 lot has 18 spaces. That is too small to score as a network commuter lot, and it was over capacity in 2019. Local buses are Guelph Transit's. The AM-peak scheduled wait from a bus to the next train toward Union is 19 minutes.

Kitchener is in the priority set. The published fare to Union is $19.40. The scheduled ride is 99 minutes, the AM headway toward Union is 30 minutes, and the generalized cost at half the shed wage is $64.36. The walk catchment has about 4,491 residents and 2,287 jobs. The largest deficit is fare burden. When I weight access more heavily, Kitchener leaves the priority set. When I weight service more heavily, Kitchener stays in the priority set.

Etobicoke North is the Kitchener station I would act on for walk access. The walkshed covers 14% of the 800 m circle and holds about 1 resident and 330 jobs. The station sits in employment land, with a station shed of about 162,309 residents and 79% of the home municipality's commuters working in Toronto.

## What I would measure next

If a local connection is shortened, I would track the scheduled AM-peak wait and, once boardings exist, boardings at that station against another station on the same line. If span of service is added, I would track off-peak and weekend boardings. I would not read a change as the effect of the intervention without accounting for return to office, construction, and the April 2024 service increase, which added trips on Lakeshore West, Lakeshore East, Kitchener, Stouffville, and Milton. That increase is an evaluation design, not a result I can estimate from open data.

## What this does not say

A weak score is a weak condition, not a low ridership number. The 2021 Census counts commuting during a period of high work from home, and I use that as a feature of the market rather than correcting it away. Parking is a 2019 snapshot. Transfer waits are scheduled, not observed. Catchments and municipal commute shares are approximations. Service and parking were supplied with ridership in mind, so I do not treat a score as a causal effect. The generalized cost uses the one published fare in the GTFS file, a stated value of time, and a scheduled wait. It is not the fare a PRESTO card charges, and it is not the cost of a whole trip from the traveller's door.
